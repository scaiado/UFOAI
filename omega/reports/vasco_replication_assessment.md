# PROJECT OMEGA — Assessment: the POSS-I Nuclear-Transient Replication

**Paper verified to exist and read directly.** arXiv:2604.00056, *"Independent
Replication of Nuclear Test-Transient Correlations and Earth Shadow Deficit in
POSS-I Photographic Plates"*, Brian Doherty, submitted 31 March 2026
(astro-ph.IM / astro-ph.EP / astro-ph.SR). It postdates this model's training
cutoff; it was fetched and read, not recalled.

## 1. What it actually claims

Replicates Bruehl & Villarroel (2025) and Villarroel et al. (2025) on
transients in Palomar Observatory Sky Survey (POSS-I) photographic plates —
point sources present on a single exposure with no counterpart in modern
surveys or temporally adjacent plates.

| finding | statistic |
|---|---|
| chi-square contingency, transients vs atmospheric nuclear tests | RR = 1.45, p = 0.011 |
| negative binomial, controlling precipitation / lunar illumination / cloud cover | all-transient IRR = **1.80** |
| same, sunlit-only subset | IRR = **3.98** |
| 10,000-iteration permutation test for temporal specificity | **p = 0.006** |
| transients inside Earth's umbral cone at geosynchronous altitude | 499 (0.46%) vs ~1.4% geometric expectation |

All transients predate Sputnik 1.

## 2. It does not conflict with Phase 2, and Phase 2 does not refute it

These are different instruments measuring different things:

| | Phase 2 (this project) | arXiv:2604.00056 |
|---|---|---|
| data | UAP **sighting reports** (human testimony catalogs) | **photographic plate transients** (astronomical emulsion) |
| comparison | detonation **date and location** | atmospheric test **date** only |
| claim tested | space-time clustering of reports near detonations | temporal excess of plate transients near tests |
| result | effect was catalog contamination; 0.6% of records produced 100% of it | effect replicates on the authors' dataset |

Phase 2's finding was specifically that **UAP catalogs contain the
detonations themselves as timeline entries** (Trinity, Hiroshima, Nagasaki,
Castle Bravo appear in 4–5 catalogs each). That failure mode cannot apply to
photographic plates. Conversely, nothing in the POSS-I work bears on whether
sighting catalogs are contaminated. **Both results can be true**, and treating
either as counter-evidence to the other is a category error.

## 3. Two caveats on the replication's strength

**3.1 It is a reanalysis, not an independent dataset.** The abstract states it
uses "the original dataset provided by the authors." That validates the
*statistics* and the *modelling choices*, which is genuinely valuable — but it
cannot detect a systematic in the upstream transient catalog itself (plate
scanning, artifact rejection, defect classification). A bias introduced at
detection survives any number of reanalyses of the same detections.

**3.2 A specific, testable mundane mechanism is not controlled for:
radioactive fallout fogging photographic emulsion.** The controls listed are
precipitation, lunar illumination and cloud cover — all atmospheric
*transmission* variables. Fallout acting directly on the emulsion is a
different channel, and it is a documented phenomenon: Kodak detected the
Trinity test in 1945 through fogged film traced to contaminated packaging
materials. Beta/gamma-active particulate settling on stored or in-process
plates produces localised developed grains that are, by construction,
single-exposure point sources with no counterpart on adjacent plates — the
exact detection signature.

### Falsifiable predictions that would separate the two hypotheses

If fallout-fogging is the mechanism:
1. The excess should scale with **test yield** and with **fallout trajectory
   over Southern California**, not merely with test occurrence.
2. It should be stronger for plates **exposed or developed within the fallout
   arrival window** (days to weeks), with a lag structure matching transport.
3. It should appear in the **plate background fog level and grain-density
   statistics**, not only in the point-source catalog.
4. It should show **no dependence on sky position**, whereas a genuine
   population of sunlit objects must.

Point 4 is where the Earth-shadow deficit becomes the discriminating
measurement rather than a secondary result: a uniform emulsion artifact cannot
produce a shadow-cone deficit, while sunlit reflectors must. That makes the
shadow result the stronger of the paper's two claims, and the one worth
attacking first. The obvious confound to rule out is whether the umbral cone
region correlates with observing geometry (airmass, elevation, time of night)
and hence with plate quality.

**None of the above has been tested here.** It is offered as the design of the
test that would settle it, not as a result. The POSS-I plate data and the
transient catalog are not reachable from this environment.

## 4. Status

This is, on the evidence, **the strongest live statistical claim in the
field** — and notably it does not come from sighting catalogs. That is
consistent with this project's own conclusion that catalogs are the wrong
instrument. It is the one external result that Project Omega's findings do not
touch, and the one most worth independent attack.
